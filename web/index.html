Your lander is ready. The launch slips three days. Does the mission still work?

At the Moon's south pole the Sun sits within 2° of the horizon, so one distant ridge can switch off a lander's power. This tool uses NASA terrain data to show which landing dates still work.

We judge every date twice. The careful estimate counts sunlight only when more than half of the Sun's disc is above the terrain, and counts Earth only when it clears the terrain by 0.5°. The generous estimate accepts a tenth of the Sun, and Earth just above the terrain. Green passes both. Yellow passes only the generous one. Red passes neither.

What if we land a little off?

—

In March 2025 the IM-2 lander came down about 250 m from its target, in a crater where sunlight could not reach its solar cells. The mission ended in about 13 hours. The ring marks that distance.

What this tool does not see

  • Rocks and small craters. The elevation map has 80 m pixels. Orbital camera images would be the next step.
  • Temperature. We report hours of darkness, which drives the cold, but we do not model heat.
  • Only 2027. Polar lighting shifts from year to year, so other years need their own run.
  • The Sun's disc is cut by a level line. A slanted ridge edge would change the visible share slightly.
  • The painted map is coarser than the single-point verdict. It checks directions every 1° instead of every 0.5°, so its count can differ by a few dates (12 against 10 at the target).
  • Earth's phase and ground stations. "Earth in view" means line of sight, not a working radio link.

NASA data this is built on

  • LOLA elevation map of the south pole, 80 m per pixel (LDEM_80S_80MPP_ADJ), from NASA Goddard's Planetary Geodesy Data Archive. It gives the terrain horizon and the 3D view.
  • JPL Horizons, for the direction of the Sun and Earth from the site for every hour of 2027.

Without either one the tool has nothing to compute. We are not affiliated with or endorsed by NASA.

Parts of the code were drafted with an AI coding assistant and then run, tested and reviewed by the team. Details are in AI_USAGE.md in the repository.
